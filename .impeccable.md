## Design Context

### Users
Developers who designed a system's architecture but had AI write the implementation. They're about to spend hours reading through potentially hundreds of files to close the gap between "my project" and "my code." The overview HTML is the lobby -- their first visual contact before a long reading journey. They need to feel grounded, oriented, and motivated to start.

### Brand Personality
Confident, minimal, developer-native. Like a well-made CLI tool that respects your intelligence. No hand-holding, no marketing speak, no dashboard bloat. Information-dense but never cluttered.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Clean and spacious, inspired by Claude's UI (claude.ai). Warm neutrals, generous whitespace, soft edges.
- **Color palette**: Pastel tones for the layer/pass palette. Warm off-white backgrounds, not stark white. Subtle color, never loud.
- **Light mode primary**, dark mode supported.
- **Anti-references**: Not a corporate dashboard. Not a GitHub readme. Not a Notion page. Not a marketing landing page.
- **Typography**: Developer-native. System fonts for body, monospace for paths and data. No decorative fonts.

### Design Principles
1. **Orientation over decoration** -- Every visual element must help the reader build a mental model. No ornamental chrome.
2. **Quiet confidence** -- The design should feel assured, not trying to impress. Muted palette, strong hierarchy, zero gratuitous effects.
3. **Data as narrative** -- Charts and numbers tell a story, not fill a dashboard. Each visualization answers a specific question the reader has.
4. **Breathe** -- Generous spacing. Let sections separate naturally. The reader just arrived; don't crowd them.
5. **Developer respect** -- Monospace where it matters. Click-to-copy on paths. No tooltips that explain the obvious. Trust the reader's intelligence.
